### The trial that quietly became a subscription

A free trial is a bet the company makes: that you will forget. The conversion is usually silent, the first charge is often the annual price, and the window to undo it closes fast. Apple's own support page says to cancel a trial subscription at least 24 hours before it ends (read 1 October 2026). Many services say the same in their terms, and almost none remind you.

Zeno is the reminder they don't send. Mark a subscription as a trial, set the end date, and three warnings arrive before the conversion, each with the price you will be charged if you do nothing. If you decide to keep it, you keep it on purpose.

### How it works

1. **Capture the trial.** When Zeno finds a receipt or statement line for a service that offers trials, it flags it. You can also add any trial by hand in a few seconds: name, end date, the price after.
2. **Get warned three times.** Seven days out, three days out, and the morning of, each with the exact post-trial amount. Quiet hours are respected.
3. **Decide once.** Keep it, and the entry becomes a normal tracked subscription. Cancel it, and the guide shows the path, including the store path if you subscribed through Apple or Google. Zeno then watches the date to confirm no charge landed.

### Why a dedicated reminder beats a calendar entry

- **It knows the amount.** A calendar event says "cancel Thing". Zeno says "Thing converts tomorrow at $119.88 for the year", because the catalog carries 480 services' prices and your receipt carries the real one.
- **It knows the store rule.** Spotify's help page says cancelling during a free trial switches you to free immediately (read 1 October 2026). Apple keeps access until the trial ends. The guide tells you which applies, so you don't cancel on day one and lose two weeks you'd paid nothing for.
- **It verifies.** After the end date, Zeno checks the next receipt or statement. No charge, no problem. A charge, and you hear about it with time to act.
- **It stays with the subscription.** If you keep the service, the reminders simply switch to renewal reminders. Nothing to re-enter.

### Zeno next to a browser extension and a notes app

Browser extensions that remind you about trials cover the trials you started in that browser, on that computer. Notes and calendars cover what you remembered to write down. Zeno covers what shows up in your receipts and statements, on the phone that gets the notification, and carries the trial through to a verified cancellation or a tracked subscription. It also costs nothing: trial reminders are in the free plan.

### Questions

**Do I have to connect a bank or a card?** No. Zeno works from receipts you scan and statements you import. No bank login required.

**What if the trial isn't in my email?** Add it by hand: name, end date, price after the trial. The reminders work the same way.

**When exactly do the reminders fire?** Seven days before the end date, three days before, and the morning of, inside the quiet hours you set.

**What if I subscribed through the App Store or Google Play?** The reminder still fires; the cancel guide shows the store's own cancellation path, which is where that trial must be cancelled.

**Does Zeno check that I wasn't charged?** Yes. After the end date it looks for a matching charge in your next receipts or imported statement and tells you either way.
